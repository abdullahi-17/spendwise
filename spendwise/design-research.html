01 Psychology

Design hypotheses to evaluate.

These ideas guide the prototype; they do not demonstrate that SpendWise changes purchasing behaviour.

Fogg Behavior Model

A prompt at a useful moment

Fogg describes motivation, ability, and a prompt coming together for a behaviour. SpendWise applies this as a hypothesis: a short, manually started check in may be useful while considering a purchase. Its timing still needs user testing.

Read Fogg's model
Self-determination theory

Keep control with the user

Self-determination theory describes autonomy, competence, and relatedness as basic psychological needs. The design interpretation here is to offer clear choices and an exit, rather than require completion.

02 Surveys & interviews

The proposed research questions.

This questionnaire and interview guide describe proposed research. Completed responses, recruitment details, and study dates are not included in this repository. The guide focuses on recent situations rather than asking participants to judge their overall habits. Camera detection remains a research topic, not a capability of the demo.

Proposed survey questionnaire (estimated 4 minutes)

  1. How old are you?Multiple choice: 18 to 24, 25 to 34, 35 to 44, 45 and over
  2. In the last 30 days, how many online purchases did you later regret or return?0, 1 to 2, 3 to 5, more than 5
  3. Think of your most recent regretted purchase. What were you doing just before?Select all: scrolling social media, stressed, bored, late at night, seeing a friend's purchase, celebrating, other
  4. At that moment, how aware were you that your mood might be affecting the decision?Not at all, slightly, aware but bought anyway, fully aware
  5. What did you do with it afterwards?Kept and used, kept unused, returned, resold
  6. Have you used a budgeting app? If you stopped, why?Never tried, felt judgemental, too much manual work, did not help, still use one
  7. Would a single question before checkout be helpful?Helpful, depends on the question, I would ignore it, annoying
  8. How would you feel about camera based mood detection if all processing stayed on your device?Comfortable, depends on the app, uncomfortable, never
  9. What would make you trust an app like this?Open text
  10. Optional: would you take part in a 20 minute follow up interview?Yes with separate contact details, no

Proposed interview guide (estimated 20 to 30 minutes)

  1. Tell me about the last thing you bought online that you were not sure about.
  2. Walk me through the hour before you bought it. Where were you, what were you doing?
  3. How were you feeling? When did you first want it?
  4. What happened after the order was placed?
  5. Have you ever tried to stop yourself from buying something? What did you try? Did it work?
  6. Show the prototype. Think aloud as you go through it with a real item you are considering.Observe where they hesitate, skip, or smile. Do not explain the interface during the task.
  7. Which part felt useful? Which part felt annoying or intrusive?
  8. What would make you delete an app like this?
Before conducting this study, obtain informed consent and avoid collecting payment details. Keep optional interview contact details separate from survey answers; responses linked to contact information are not anonymous. Explain that this is a design study, and that participation and camera use are optional.
03 Research

Research evidence and open questions.

Survey results are not reported here because a source dataset, sample size, and study date are not available in this repository. The following questions need validation; they are not presented as interview findings.

When is a check in welcome?

Ask participants whether a manual reflection is useful while considering a purchase, and when it feels distracting.

Which language feels neutral?

Compare descriptions of mood, planning, and urgency without labelling the person or the purchase.

What earns trust?

Explore preferences around optional camera access, disclosure of data use, and the ability to leave immediately.

Does the pause help?

Observe completion, skipping, and reported usefulness. A changed purchase decision alone does not establish a lasting benefit.

04 Personas

Illustrative personas for three scenarios.

These illustrative personas are design scenarios, not verified participant profiles. Their backgrounds and behaviours help explore different needs; the features described are proposed mobile capabilities.

Jordan, 24

Recent graduate, first finance job. Stress spender.

Context

  • Spends most on Sunday evenings after a hard week
  • Feeds are the discovery channel
  • Deleted two budgeting apps within three weeks

Proposed support

  • Names the emotion without judgement
  • Connects a purchase to the travel goal, no blocking
  • Catches the Sunday pattern early

Maya, 34

Senior designer, two kids. Boredom and comparison spender.

Context

  • Browses on work breaks and after bedtime
  • High return rate, does not feel emotional while buying
  • Finds finance tools condescending

Proposed support

  • Surfaces patterns she cannot see, framed as insight
  • Makes the cooling period feel like a feature
  • Shows her own return history

Devon, 41

Sole proprietor, Shopify seller. Reward spender.

Context

  • Business and personal spend blur together
  • Spends after both good and bad months
  • Data driven, dislikes being lectured

Proposed support

  • Frames the check as insight, not restriction
  • Separates emotional from intentional spend over time
  • Doubles as a merchant integration target

07 Design validation

Phase III: testing the purchase reflection.

Earlier project material describes a purchase questionnaire and a follow-up about its perceived effect. The reconstruction below contains seven participant rows. The original study report, source image, raw responses, recruitment details, and dates are not included in this checkout, so these historical results have not been independently verified here.

Objective

Evaluate the early scoring approach in real shopping situations, and understand whether the reflection changes a participant’s decision or provides a perceived benefit.

Method described in the study

Participants complete a questionnaire while shopping online, before making a purchase. They then answer a follow-up questionnaire about the experience and its effect on their decision.

The test sequence

Participant results: visual reconstruction

The labels are interpreted as follows: Impulsivity represents the initial purchase rating; Effect represents reported influence on the shopping decision; and Benefit represents perceived usefulness. These interpretations are inferred from the supplied objective and findings, not confirmed questionnaire definitions.

Findings attributed to earlier study material

01 · Decision influence

The score did not always predict the decision

The study summary reports that higher impulsivity levels did not consistently correspond to a changed purchase decision.

02 · Perceived value

Most participants saw a benefit

The summary reports a perceived benefit in the app and its functions. It does not provide an exact count or percentage for this finding.

03 · Reflection

Lower scores did not rule out an effect

Participants described as having low impulsivity could still report that the questionnaire influenced their purchasing decision.

04 · Confidence in results

Further validation was needed

The summary calls for more testing. These findings do not establish a lasting reduction in spending, returns, or regret.

What this means for the interface

The earlier summary motivates investigating reflection separately from scoring; it does not establish effectiveness. The current design direction uses a short, neutral reflection and leaves the decision with the user. The earlier “smart” and “safe” labels should not be read as demonstrated judgements of a purchase.

Evidence boundaries

The historical chart is retained as an explicitly unverified reconstruction. Before using it as research evidence, provide the original source and confirm consent, recruitment, dates, definitions, and calculations. Functional browser/API tests are separate evidence and do not validate behavioural outcomes.

The supplied chart labels three series: Impulsivity, Effect, and Benefit. It does not establish their units, questionnaire wording, calculation method, or scale definitions. The visual reconstruction above preserves approximate bar proportions only; it does not supply numerical comparisons or resolve whether the three series share a scale. The summary also does not provide recruitment details, study dates, or longer-term follow-up; those details are needed to assess the findings more fully.
